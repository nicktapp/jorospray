How to spot one

  • Females: yellow and blue-gray banded bodies
  • A red patch underneath
  • Golden webs, often strung across porches and eaves

Why they spread

In spring, young Joros balloon on silk threads to new spots. They tolerate cold, traffic noise and city life.

Should you worry?

Mostly a nuisance, not a danger.

Their bite is rarely an issue, experts say.

Where they've spread

From one I-85 exit to five states in about a decade.

  1. 2014

    First confirmed in the US near Braselton and Hoschton, GA, along I-85.

  2. 2022

    Roughly 75 miles in every direction. Atlanta numbers take off.

  3. 2025

    Joro numbers in Atlanta doubled in three years (AJC).

  4. 2026

    Now

    Established in Georgia, South Carolina, North Carolina, Tennessee and Alabama, with isolated populations in Maryland, Virginia and Pennsylvania. Researchers expect them across most of the eastern US.
